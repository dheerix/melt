# MELT Master

This is the living source of truth for MELT's product and menu thinking. Detailed category work lives under `docs/menu/`; recipes, supplier data, exact costs and sensitive operational data stay outside public Git.

## Brand premise

**MELT** is an eggless bakery built around the idea: **Purely eggless. Just enough sweet.**

The menu should not behave like one giant list. Products are organized as product worlds, then marketed through the moments in which people naturally buy them.

Core principle: **Product → Occasion → Customer.**

Rather than positioning products as “healthy,” prefer language such as **wholesome**, ingredient-forward, familiar, restrained and “just enough sweet.” Avoid unnecessary nutritional promises.

### 5. Bento Cakes
Bento is a full MELT category, not merely a cake size.

**Concept:** tiny personal celebrations.

Working thought: **For one. For two. For no reason.**

Use cases include birthdays for two, anniversaries, congratulations, apologies, new jobs, exam results, friendship, date night, child's mini birthday and “just because” moments.

Celebration flavours may be offered in Bento format where technically suitable. Designs, messages and custom themes form part of the product experience.

## Product concept framework

Every menu item should eventually answer:
1. What exactly are we selling?
2. Why does it belong at MELT?
3. Who naturally buys it?
4. What moment triggers the purchase?
5. What makes MELT's version recognizable?
6. What formats/sizes should it use?
7. How should it look in photography/packaging?
8. What is its short selling line?
9. Is it a repeat or occasion purchase?
10. Is it permanent, seasonal, festival-led or limited?

## Menu architecture

Customer-facing menus should be curated rather than expose every capability at once. The capability menu can remain broad while launch/seasonal menus highlight a smaller set of heroes.

The working customer-facing hierarchy is:
**Everyday Bakes · Celebrations · Cheesecakes & Desserts · Bento · Chocolates**
